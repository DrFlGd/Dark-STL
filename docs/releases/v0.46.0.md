**Thin parts keep their shape when displaced, and a Thickness view shows what is too thin to print.**

## What changed
- **Thin parts no longer clip.** Displacing used to give a claw tip, a blade's edge or a cape as much relief as the texture asked for, even when that was more than the part is thick or than the gap to the body behind it, so its sides were pushed through each other. Now Displace measures how much room every point has, behind it (inside the part) and in front of it (the gap to the next surface), and lowers the relief smoothly where it would come close to using it all. Thick parts and open areas keep all their relief. Where a part is thinner than the points are apart (the very edge of a blade, the last millimetre of a point), the few places still folding through are found and smoothed out.
- **Displace › More settings** has the two controls: **Keep thin parts from clipping** (on by default) and **Room the relief may use** (80 % by default: relief may use four fifths of the gap or thickness; lower keeps thin parts thicker, 100 % lets it come right up to the surface). The **Enhanced** panel says how many points were lowered and by how much, and how much of the relief was kept.
- **Thickness shading.** Beside Textured, Clay and Wireframe, the 3D view has **Thickness**: the model coloured by how thick it is behind each point. Red is thinner than the thinnest wall your printer makes, orange to yellow up to twice and four times it, grey is thick enough. Type the height you'll print it at in the small panel over the view (empty: the size it is in the game) and the thinnest wall (0.8 mm unless you change it), and the colours change at once; the panel says how much of the model is too thin. Single sheets with no thickness of their own, like capes and banners, show red. Where game parts overlap, the hidden surfaces inside another part don't count.
- **Command line:** `darkstl enhance` keeps thin parts too, and says what it did; `--no-thin-parts` switches it off and `--thin-room 60` sets the room the relief may use.

Tested on synthetic scenes built for it: a 4 mm blade, three curled 60 mm claws and a cape 3 mm behind a body, all with strong relief. At 70,000 points the triangles passing through each other went from thousands (blade and claws) and hundreds (cape) to none, keeping 87 to 98 % of the relief; at 1 million points keeping thin parts adds 7 to 9 seconds to the Displace step. The relief on flat and thick test panels is unchanged. The Thickness view and the new Displace controls are tested in Chromium.

## Windows
- **`DarkSTL_0.46.0_x64-setup.exe`** installs Dark STL for your account (no administrator rights). It isn't signed, so SmartScreen asks once: *More info > Run anyway*.
- **`DarkSTL_0.46.0_x64-portable.zip`** is the same app in a folder: unzip it anywhere and run `Dark STL.exe`.
