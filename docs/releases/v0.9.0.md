Models can now be **cut into pieces** for printing. A new **Cut** tab places cut planes in the 3D view; the model splits into watertight pieces, and each cut gets its own joint (flat, pegs, dowels, magnet pockets or a key) with its own clearance.

## What changed
- **Cut tab.** *Add a cut* across X, Y or Z, or *Facing me* (facing you as the view is turned). The plane shows in the 3D view as a translucent square through the model: drag it to slide it, drag its round handle to tilt it, or use *Pick a point* and click the model to put it there. The tab also has the position (slider and millimetres), tilt and turn, and a list of the cuts (select or remove). Cuts are remembered per model.
- **Joints and clearance, per cut.**
  - *Flat*: the cut faces as they are, to glue.
  - *Pegs*: round pegs on one side (switchable) and holes on the other, wider by twice the clearance and deeper by it. Diameter, depth and how many (automatic puts two where they fit, so the pieces can't turn).
  - *Dowels*: holes on both sides, and the dowels themselves as extra pieces (or holes only, for bought pins or filament, such as 1.75 mm).
  - *Magnets*: pockets on both sides for disc magnets of a given diameter and thickness.
  - *Key*: a tongue shaped like the cut face, inset from its edge, into a matching groove, so the pieces only fit one way.
  - Each area of the cut face (two legs cut by one plane, say) gets its own features. Where a piece is too thin for one, it is made shorter or left out, and the tab says where the cut was left flat. *Wall around joints* (1.6 mm) sets the least material kept around them.
- **Watertight pieces from game models.** Game meshes are open shells with overlapping parts, so the model is made solid before it is cut: small openings are closed, open surfaces such as capes are thickened (*Open surfaces*, 1.2 mm), broken parts are rebuilt, and the parts are joined into one solid. The tab notes what was done. The solid is kept, so moving a cut only cuts again, and the last three sets of pieces are kept like generated models.
- **Pieces in the 3D view.** *Show › Pieces* shows each piece in its own colour; *Spread apart* moves them away from each other to see the joints. The tab lists each piece's size, volume and triangles.
- **Export pieces.** Export has a *Pieces* choice: each piece goes to its own file in a `<name>_pieces` folder, laid on its biggest flat cut face (one without pegs or a tongue on it), ready to print. Switch off *Lay each piece on a cut face* to keep them where they are in the model.
- **Command line.** `--cut AXIS=MM[:JOINT]` on `export` and `enhance` (repeatable, `z=50%` for halfway, joints `flat`, `pegs`, `dowels`, `magnets`, `key`) with `--clearance` writes the pieces as `<output>_pieceN`.
- **Engine.** New `cut` and `cut_state` commands, `model_mesh` with `which=pieces`, and `export` with `which=pieces`. `manifold3d` is a new dependency (included in the Windows app).

## Windows
- **`DarkSTL_0.9.0_x64-setup.exe`** installs Dark STL for your account (no administrator rights). It isn't signed, so SmartScreen asks once: *More info > Run anyway*.
- **`DarkSTL_0.9.0_x64-portable.zip`** is the same app in a folder: unzip it anywhere and run `Dark STL.exe`.

## Other systems
Python 3.10 or newer. Download the wheel below, then:

```
pip install "dark_stl-0.9.0-py3-none-any.whl[gui,remesh,fast,ai]"
darkstl
```
