**Cyberpunk weapons get a card from their names when their appearance files give none**, and the app says what it found.

## What changed
- **Weapon cards from the game's naming.** Cyberpunk names a weapon's meshes `w_revolver__darra_nova__base1`, `w_revolver__darra_nova__mag1`, `w_revolver__darra_nova__scope1`... and keeps them all in one archive. The name guessing (0.15.0) grouped by folder and skipped folders with more than 48 files, and didn't take `base1` for a base, so no Cyberpunk weapon got a card that way. Meshes are now grouped by the name before the double underscore wherever they sit, `base1`/`base1_01`/`body2` count as bases, and each base of a weapon (`base1`, `base2`) gets its own card sharing the parts. These cards say *Guessed*; a weapon whose appearance file (`.app`) is read keeps the card made from it (0.20.0).
- **What the appearance files gave.** The Home page's note for a Cyberpunk folder now says how many weapon appearance files were read, how many weapons and attachments they named, how many became configurable, and the first error if any; the engine log (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\DarkSTL\cache\darkstl.log`) has the details.
- **The unnamed appearance is called `default`.** Many Cyberpunk meshes leave their first appearance without a name, which showed as a blank entry at the top of the appearance list (the dropdown at the top right of a Cyberpunk model). It is now listed as `default`.
- **Cyberpunk textures land where the game puts them.** Two faults put the Darra Nova's finishes in the wrong places. The game's V runs up the texture while Dark STL's runs down, so every Cyberpunk mesh had its textures upside down against its UVs; the mesh's V is now flipped on import. And a weapon draws each finish through two masks (first-person `masksset`, third-person `maskssettpp`) that shared one texture slot, so every chunk got the last mask's blend; the blends are now kept per setup and mask. Checked against the game: the Doom Doom revolver now has its dark red grip, red trigger and black frame.
- **Dark game textures no longer show light and washed out.** On some graphics drivers and webviews the viewer's colour map was drawn without its sRGB decoding, so a near-black Cyberpunk weapon finish (Darra Nova *Doom Doom*) came out light grey with white marbling. The viewer now decodes the map in its own shader, which gives the same picture on every GPU: that revolver renders black with its dark red grip, as in the game.
- **Finely tiled layers average instead of sparkling.** A layer of a multilayered material whose texture tiles many times across the model (brushed steel, grit) was sampled one texel per output texel and came out speckled; it is now averaged to the output's texel size. The mapping of the blended colour and normal maps was checked on a real revolver (`w_revolver__darra_nova__base1_01`): the mask layers land on the right parts and the viewer shows it in the game's colours.
- **Collect keeps a weapon configurable.** Collecting a weapon from the archives now copies its `.app` and `.ent` beside its mesh, materials, layer setups, masks and textures, so the collected folder gives the same card (and a collected weapon is a complete sample to send along).

## Windows
- **`DarkSTL_0.22.3_x64-setup.exe`** installs Dark STL for your account (no administrator rights). It isn't signed, so SmartScreen asks once: *More info > Run anyway*.
- **`DarkSTL_0.22.3_x64-portable.zip`** is the same app in a folder: unzip it anywhere and run `Dark STL.exe`.
